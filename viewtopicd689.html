I used two active antennas with the gp-02 module and neither had good results, one of the antennas did not receive any satellite signal. I had to solve the problem in an artisanal and empirical way, starting from the idea that our hobby (amateur radio) is an experiment... The antenna that comes with the gp-02 is passive and not active, which is why I used a small short wire and a mesh to serve as an antenna. The small wire that also serves as an antenna added to the coaxial mesh, which in addition to helping with reception, has capacitive interaction with the equipment structure (HT) favors better reception. It was the best way found to obtain the best satellite signals in non-ideal conditions.

You should measure the actual current taken by the GPS module, because the Maximum current from a single pin on an official STM32 is 25mA.
You should also measure any transient current spikes.
There is also a maximum total current from all pins, which I don't know off the top of my head.
i.e you can't take 25mA from all pins as that would overload the whole CPU.
Albeit I don't think any of the other pins is likely to be loaded with more than a few mA, but we don't know this for sure.

I had a quick look at the online references / data sheets for the GO-02, but the current consumption seemed to be different in the various data sheets that you can find.

I'm also slighly sceptical about the claimed current consumption of 25 or 30mA

You should measure this youself and make sure you are confident that you won't be overloading, the pin, as if you blow that pin, it can take out other pins at the same time and potentially make your radio useless, as it won't work if any of the CPU pins stop working.
