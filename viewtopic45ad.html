Its almost impossible to fix a problem which does not occur for any of the developers.

Possibly some other calibration setting which the official firmware is applying to the hardware, which is only applied to some radios, i.e. We send a lot of unknown data to the DMR chip and also to the RF chip.

There are many undocumented parts of both the DMR and RF chip, so possibly the data that we recoded on our radios, is not correct for your radio.

The only way to confirm this would be for you to dissassemble your radio and solder wires inside and then capture all the traffic to both the DMR chip and also the RF chip, but this is very difficult and would potentially damage your radio.

I almost destroyed my MD2017 when solding wires to the CPU / MCU chip to capture the data to the RF chip, becuase I shorted multiple pins
Luckily I saw the short before applying power to the radio and was able to remove the solder using brade wick.

Others have not been so lucky, at least one person may have destroyed their radio by shorting the internal electronics while trying to attach a GPS.
I still don't know whether they managed to fix that radio

The codec is the same in all the radios, from the single band MD380 onwards. (Note we don't support the MD380 because it uses an older version of DMR chip)

The reason we were able to use the codec is only becasue Travis Goodspeed found and analysed the codec in the MD380, and we were able to use his analysis to find the location of the codec in the other radios.

It is too complicated for us to use the codec from each individual radio, because when the location of the codec changes in the official file, it requires changes to both the CPS and the firmware to use the codec in the different location, and as the codec binary is identical this would be a pointless waste of time and effort.

All firmware for both TYT and Baofeng radios is written by the same external software development organisation, the write the codec and the application firmware and also the bootloader for all the TYT and Baofeng radios, which we support.
We believe that this 'organisation' is a state run company, rather than a private enterprise and hence has no company name

I see its been a couple of months since the last reply to this thread, but, instead of starting a new one, I thought I should post here first.
I just recently purchased a UV390 w/gps. I am using OpenGD77 and have this same "Rx Missing" issue. I am using a Raspberry Pi 3b duplex hotspot at home as there are no repeaters near me. For mobile, I use a Pi Zero 2w also duplex with a Tmobile wifi hotspot. The problem is the same regardless of which hotspot or wifi connection I am using.
For me, it does not occur during a receive but rather, between contacts. Here is an example,, if I am monitoring a qso, when one person finishes and un-keys the mic, when the next person keys up and starts talking, I see the signal being received on the rssi bar, but no audio is coming out of the speaker. If I give the channel knob a quick click one direction or the other and back again, or a quick tap on the ptt, the audio then comes out the speaker. This is random and not a consistent issue. It may happen 2-3 times during a conversation, or not at all. I have tried putting the 868 on the same channel and when the 390 drops the audio, the 868 does not. It is very inconvenient to have to have two radios going because the one is unreliable. It is also inconvenient to have to constantly monitor the rssi bar to see if anyone has started talking and I'm just not hearing it.
Some things I have tried: updated to firmware 20240412, no difference. Various filters, dmr, ts, etc on or off,, no change. Eco positions, no change. Dmr crc on or off (not sure what it does) no change. Changed my Tx and Rx freqs to have a wider separation.. no change.
I really like the firmware and the cps is much more intuitive to my brain than the anytone is. I hope the information I have given as to how and when it occurs is helpful to the developers. If you need any more info, I would be happy to assist.
Thank you and 73
