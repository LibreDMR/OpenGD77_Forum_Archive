OK, here is the dirty way of making it work, hopefully.

First watch this video. It is how to use the calibration on the Pi-Star hotspot.

https://youtu.be/n81MCz4VcSs?si=CpREL1KOzTfX7uH9

Now, because you have two radio. you will have to find a compromise, a sweet spot so to speak.
We know the hotspot work with your old radio, so we will start calibration with your new radio.

calibrate to the best with your new radio, then take note of the offset number. Write it down, but don't click save.

Now Calibrate using your old radio, Take note of the offset.

Then use the offset in the middle. For example if the offset for new radio is +100 and the offset for the old radio is -50. Then the middle spot is +25. click save. and stop.

Now hope fully the hotspot will work with bout of your radio.

Hope this help.

 The only way to align the frequency calibration is to use a SDR. Transmit on 435MHz FM on your other radio , and zoom in using your SDR to look at the centre frequency of the transmission.
When you have centered your SDR display on the middle of the freq, with the SDR zoomed in as far as it will go. Then go into the calibration screen of the OpenGD77 radio, and in the UHF / 435MHz freq cal page, adjust the freq until the transmission is exactly centred where the other radio was transmitting.

Post by G4EML » Thu Dec 05, 2024 5:40 pm

Yes, an accurate counter would work OK.

The problem is due to the poor receiver performance in most hotspots. They cannot cope well with even small frequency errors and have to be fine tuned to match the radio in use. Hence if more than one radio is used they all need to be very closely matched to each other in frequency.

A proper commercial repeater (or a hotspot built with an OpenGD77 radio) does not have this problem and has a much better tolerance to the normal small frequency errors between different radios.
