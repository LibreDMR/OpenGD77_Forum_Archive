Why do you think you need to calibrate the radio.

Many people never bother to calibrate the power or frequency

The factory power calibration is often inaccurate, by +/- 20%, but in normal use this does not make a noticeable difference

Frequency calibration is also normally ok, albeit perhaps +/-500 Hz accuracy

Frequency calibration is normally only needed when people have multiple radios and use a hotspot.
Hotspots are normally adjusted to match the radio, but of someone has multiple radios, then the hotspot is calibrated to one radio, and the other radios are calibrated to match that radio

just read your first post.

If you already calibrated your hotspot to your other radio, or it just happened to work with your other radio, then you will need to calibrate this new radio to be on exactly the same frequency as this new radio

Hotspots have a very narrow frequency acceptance window, unlike normal repeaters, becuase the RF chip used in hotspots is not designed for DMR.

The only way to align the frequency calibration is to use a SDR. Transmit on 435MHz FM on your other radio , and zoom in using your SDR to look at the centre frequency of the transmission.
When you have centered your SDR display on the middle of the freq, with the SDR zoomed in as far as it will go. Then go into the calibration screen of the OpenGD77 radio, and in the UHF / 435MHz freq cal page, adjust the freq until the transmission is exactly centred where the other radio was transmitting.

If you don't have an SDR dongle, borrow one from your local club, or buy one, they are quite cheap and the PC software is free
