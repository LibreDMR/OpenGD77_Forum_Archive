de KC1VXD
Does it drop power even on the lower power settings ?
Yes, it does. I was running it at 4W because at 5W it showed spurious emissions that were not present at lower power. All was well for multiple months of intermittent use. Now, if I have had it off for a while and turn it on and test it immediately it will output 4W initially but after a few seconds it drops to the milliwatt range and stays that way; I don't know if there is a component overheating, some sort of protection circuitry, or what. Starting at 1W doesn't prevent the power drop from happening. I haven't tried at ultra-low outputs but maybe I should. It still produces a transmission that is usable at quite short range even when the output is only 1mW, though! So I can maybe still set it up as a hotspot...

One thing I did discover, after the fact, is that the rubber duckie antenna that comes with it has an SWR of like 14 at VHF frequencies (I had initially tested the power output with a TinySA and an in-line attenuator but now have a SWR meter/power meter). So I do wonder if that could have been the reason it stopped working. At some point when I have time I'm going to take the radio apart and see if I can figure anything out that's fixable, but it's doubtful I will be able to fix it.

I like the platform well enough that I will definitely be buying another radio. The GPS, for me, is a game changer when travelling (and it's good enough that I'm considering getting the mobile TYT 9600 although it seems like the implementation for that radio is not as good as for the handhelds.

It's not possible to test a "rubber duckie" in a good way on a VNA or antenna analyzer/swr meter in a proper way.
You need to create an earth plane that is similar to the radio including a person who is holding the radio.

But it's not a perfect match when using the radio normally either, the swr is probably around 3:1 or so on most frequencies.

I have also tested some duckies on my TinySA but I at least noticed some resonance points where the swr was low.
