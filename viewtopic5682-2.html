Hello all,

I was hoping for some advice on my MD-9600. I bought it at an estate sale (silent key) for a reasonable price. It's already programmed for the area that I live in. I want to use the CPS software to make some changes. Unfortunately, it seems like the USB port is dead.

I've used a standard USB cable, a TYT-PROG-9600 cable, and another TYT COM-based cable. Using the first two, the host computer (Windows or Mac) does not respond. There is no indication that a USB device has been plugged in. I've used a combination of GUI and terminal apps to query the USB controllers. The COM-based cable creates a COM port in Windows Device Manager, but I now know it is the wrong cable type.

I've opened the case a couple of times. The board is V5. Recently, I opened it to see if I could find any damage related to the USB port. I work in IT at a school, and it's not unusual for a USB port to become damaged if stressed. I noticed a capacitor (470) on traces leading to the antenna connection. It had a white substance around the cap, but after scrapping it away, it didn't appear the capacitor was blown. There is white thermal paste in other parts of the radio. Maybe some migrated over there.

I didn't take the board entirely out, as I found the antenna connector is soldered to the board and would have to be desoldered to remove it. The USB port is on the bottom of the board.

The radio works fine otherwise.

Has anyone here had experience with a failed USB port on the MD-9600? Is there a way to disable it via software?

Thanks for your help.

Post by G4EML » Tue Jun 25, 2024 8:40 am

I can confirm that the TYT programming cable is just a standard USB cable. Any USB A to USB Mini cable should work.

You may need to check the soldering of the USB socket. That is the most likely failure point.

If you need to remove the PCB to do this it is fairly easy. Just remove lots of screws, unplug the fan and speaker and unsolder the short wire to the antenna connector. The board should then lift out with the power cable attached.
Note that the screws are not all the same length, so make a note of where they come from.

I desoldered the antenna connections and pulled the PCB. Thermal paste is everywhere on the bottom of the board. I used a known-good USB cable and pulled the USB-A enclosure back enough to get my multimeter probe in and use the test points on the board. Unfortunately, the data pins both worked. So, the data makes it to the board, which means it's likely a failed surface mount component on the PCB.

I reassembled the radio and tested it. It still works fine for TX/RX on the channels it's programmed for. Oh well. It's still a good radio. I'll learn how to program it manually. I may pick up a new one to experiment with OpenGD77 at some point.
